# Ayura AI — Gym Knowledge Base Review Packet

_For a clinical exercise professional: a physiotherapist, sports physician, or
BAMS practitioner with exercise-prescription training. Estimated effort: ~1 day._

## Why this exists

The gym feature's engine logic is independently tested — 250+ automated tests
cover the programming, the load prescription, the scheduling and the safety
gating, and a 4,950-plan sweep across eleven profile types confirms that no
exercise carrying a contraindication ever reaches a practitioner who declared
that condition.

**What none of that can certify is whether the contraindications are right.**

The library holds 902 exercises. **18** carry `contraindications_reviewed`. The
other **884** have safety tags that were generated by rules, and no clinically
qualified person has read them.

That is the last thing standing between this feature and launch, and it is the
one thing engineering cannot do for itself.

## Why this is a day and not a week

884 rows reviewed one at a time is data entry, and nobody finishes it.

But the tags came from **rules**, so their errors are systematic rather than
random. Every fault found so far has been a rule that fired on some of a class
and not the rest. So the reviewable unit is the rule, not the row.

`gym_mechanism_review.csv` groups the library by mechanism — what the movement is
and what it trains — and reports how consistently each contraindication is
applied across that group:

| movement_class | muscle | contraindication | tagged | coverage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| deadlift | legs | herniated_disc | 44/52 | 85% |
| deadlift | legs | heart_disease | 34/52 | 65% |
| overhead_press | shoulders | hypertension | 3/13 | 23% |

A group tagged 70/70 is a rule that fired everywhere. A group tagged 3/13 is a
rule that half-fired, **and one of those two answers is wrong.** 116 of the 242
rules land in that ambiguous band; they are sorted to the top of the file, largest
group first, because that is where a wrong answer reaches the most people.

For each flagged row, three columns:

- **`tag_correct_for_this_movement__Y_N`** — should this class of movement carry
  this contraindication at all?
- **`coverage_correct__Y_N`** — should it apply to *all* of the group, or only the
  ones currently tagged? The `examples_tagged` and `examples_untagged` columns show
  you which fell on each side.
- **`missing_contraindications`** — what this class should carry and does not.

`gym_exercise_review.csv` has all 902 rows for spot-checking anything the grouping
does not settle. It is a reference, not a worklist.

## Priority order

1. **The 116 rows marked `REVIEW`**, top to bottom. Stop whenever you run out of
   time — they are ordered by how many practitioners a wrong answer reaches.
2. **The four decisions below**, which engineering made and which need a
   clinician's confirmation.
3. **Cardiovascular tagging as a whole.** Three separate faults have been found
   in it and it is the mechanism with the worst consequences. It deserves a pass
   of its own even if the sampled rules look fine.

## Faults already found, and what was done

These were found by inspecting roughly a dozen of the 884 rules. That hit rate is
the reason this review is necessary; please treat them as a sample, not a list.

| Fault | Was | Now |
|---|---|---|
| All 179 barbell exercises marked `pitta: avoid` — the tag was derived from the *equipment*, so a Vata or Pitta practitioner could never be shown a barbell squat, deadlift or bench press | equipment-derived | re-derived to neutral; Vata's real aversion (explosive/irregular work) and Kapha's (passive work) are movement properties and were already correct |
| Maximal-effort conditioning carried no cardiovascular tag, while a moderate dance class was flagged for both `heart_disease` and `hypertension` | Sprint Intervals, HIIT Circuit, assault bike, burpees, battle ropes, jump rope, mountain climbers all untagged | all seven tagged `hypertension` + `heart_disease` |
| 13 foam-rolling (`-Smr`) entries filed as `strength`, so they were prescribed as main lifts with a weight range | `category: strength` | `category: stretching`, excluded from main work |
| `Elliptical Trainer` and `Mountain Climbers` each existed twice with contradictory safety data | two rows, disagreeing | merged, contraindications unioned |

## Four decisions needing your confirmation

1. **The surviving `Elliptical Trainer` keeps `pregnancy_safe: true`.** The
   duplicate it was merged with said `false`, but that `false` came from the same
   rule-derived template that also called an elliptical a strength exercise
   prescribed in sets of 12–15 reps. Low-impact supported cardio is ordinarily
   among the better prenatal options. **Confirm or reverse.**

2. **`hypertension` is used as the age proxy for cardiovascular strain**, the way
   `osteoporosis` is used for axial loading. It means a practitioner over 60
   is kept away from breath-holding strain work and maximal conditioning whether
   or not they have declared a cardiovascular condition. **Confirm the threshold
   (60) and the mechanism.**

3. **Impact work is withheld at obese BMI classifications** — jumping, skipping,
   running, at every training age. Resistance work is untouched. The reasoning is
   landing force through the knee and ankle. **Confirm, and say whether it should
   also apply at `overweight`.**

4. **Prenatal training is declared unsupported.** 56 exercises are pregnancy-safe,
   about 13 reach a beginner after the goal and level gates, and 40 of the 56 are
   stretches. The plan is generated but carries a notice saying it is not a
   prenatal programme and to work with a prenatal-qualified instructor.
   **Confirm that declining to serve this properly is the right call** — the
   alternative is sourcing a real prenatal exercise library.

## What happens to your answers

Every `N` with a correction goes back into `gym_exercises.json` and is covered by
a regression test, so it cannot silently revert. Rows you confirm get
`contraindications_reviewed: true`, which is the field this packet exists to
raise from 18.

Regenerate this packet after any KB change:

```bash
python scripts/build_gym_review_packet.py
```
